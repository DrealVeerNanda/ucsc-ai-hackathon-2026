Why not vibration?

74%

of overlapping buzzes were misread

Yeganeh, University of Iceland 2026: 26% read correctly when buzzes overlapped, 93% one at a time.

Vibration needs a motor per direction spread over the body (a wrist band, a belt, a face rig), and the buzzes overlap: when buzzes overlapped, 74% were misread (26% read correctly, vs 93% one at a time). A face rig has the same problem with more motors. Even single wrist buzzes were felt in the wrong spot 1 time in 10 (Trant et al., Sci Rep 2025, 41 blind participants), and spoken cues gave faster grasps. One word in an earbud needs no decoding.

See: iPhone color + lidar to the GX10. Find: YOLO-World finds the mug, MediaPipe the fingertip. Measure: lidar turns both into 3D points. Speak: the biggest gap becomes one word in the earbuds.

The core loop

  1. pixel and lidar depth to 3D
  2. where the hand must go
  3. one word: right, down, forward
  4. then “grab”
Median depth over a small patch, ignoring holes. Table plane by RANSAC. Words at most twice a second. About 35 ms per frame on the GX10.
